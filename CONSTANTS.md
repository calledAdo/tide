# Constants, deployment values, and simulation output

The [README](README.md) states the rules. This file holds the numbers behind them: what is fixed in the
contract, what a deployer chooses, what the contract derives, and what the simulations produce.

Nothing here is a tuning knob after deployment. Every fixed value is a compile-time constant in
[`contract/src/sea.rs`](contract/src/sea.rs), and the contract account holds no access keys, so none of
them can be changed on a live sea.

---

## 1. Fixed in the contract

Identical on every sea anyone deploys.

| Rule | Fixed contract rule |
| --- | --- |
| Initial capacity | Starts at 1,000,000 fish before generation decay |
| Biology | Initial stock 700,000 fish; Allee threshold 120,000; `r = 0.6` |
| Fleet cap | The whole fleet at full effort takes at most 25% of opening stock |
| Effort and fuel | Effort is 1-10,000 basis points; fuel is `0.002 * stake * effort`, so full effort burns `0.002 * stake` |
| Quota | 80% of the current capacity's maximum sustainable yield |
| Boat deposit | 1,000 TIDE minimum stake; 1% of each deposit burns |
| Assembly | Every 3 seasons; up to 3 names per shard; 40% quorum; TIDE burned per name written |
| Exile | Lasts 10 seasons and leaves the stake in the fleet denominator |
| Wreck | Burns 50% of every boat's stake and returns 50% to its wallet |
| Reseed | Costs 1,000,000 TIDE, must fund within 60 seasons, and starts at 70% of new capacity |
| Recovery and decay | Capacity falls 20% per generation; recovery burns 50% of catch for 30 seasons |
| Fallback | An unfunded return starts at the larger of 240,000 fish or 30% of new capacity |
| Settlement | Each call settles at most 40 elapsed seasons |

### Why these three matter most

**Allee threshold, 120,000 fish.** Below a critical density a real population's growth turns negative,
so collapse is a one-way door rather than a slow decline. It is what makes the endgame a decision
instead of arithmetic. It never moves, in any generation.

**Quota at 80% of maximum sustainable yield.** Maximum sustainable yield is the largest catch the sea
can replace in a season. Fishing exactly at it leaves no margin for error, so the fleet's share is set
below it.

**Capacity decay, 20% per generation.** The sea must come back or the game is finite and unravels from
the end backwards; it must come back worse or a collapse costs nothing. Because the 120,000 floor stays
put while the ceiling falls, the safe band narrows every generation and a community can still lose for
good.

## 2. Set when a sea is deployed

Three values, supplied once to the constructor.

| Value | This deployment |
| --- | --- |
| `genesis` | Six generated demo boat accounts (`ana`, `ben`, `cy`, `dee`, `eve`, `greedy`), each with 10,000,000 TIDE |
| `season_len_sec` | 60 seconds |
| `faucet_amount` | 100,000 TIDE per account; zero on a real launch |

A deposit burns 1%, so a 10,000,000 TIDE genesis deposit becomes a 9,900,000 TIDE boat.

---

## 3. Derived by the contract

Capacity, maximum sustainable yield, quota, and each boat's fair share move together. None of these is
a stored limit; each is computed from the sea's current carrying capacity.

| Generation | Capacity | MSY | Quota (80% of MSY) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Generation 0 | 1,000,000 | 73,155 | 58,524 |
| After one wreck | 800,000 | 55,453 | 44,362 |
| After two wrecks | 640,000 | 41,331 | 33,064 |

A boat's fair share is `quota * stake / fleet`.

with a 3.44x edge; the two-stage rule removes the edge entirely.

### The reckoning — `python3 sim/tide_reckoning.py`

How much a raider's final grab costs it, measured against what the wreck it causes burns from its own
stake.

| Opening stock | Wreck loss versus final grab |
| ---: | ---: |
| 700,000 fish | 265x |
| 300,000 fish | 618x |
| 120,000 fish, the line | 1,545x |
| Across three generations | 978x |

The ratio rises as the stock falls, so the closer the sea is to the line, the worse the last grab is
for the raider. That is what flips the endgame from racing to grab into pulling back.
